Research paper

Steering a 3B Code Model Selects One Known Algorithm Rather Than Diversifying

Preregistered experiments on Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, with open prompts for path search and sorting.

Raising the temperature, rewording the prompt and steering a model's activations are all used to get less obvious solutions. This paper asks whether they change the solution idea or only its wording. It labels the algorithm in every program Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct writes for Implement a sorting algorithm and Implement a path search algorithm, and runs each program against a test suite. Steering the model away from its default algorithm does not spread its choices over the algorithms it knows. It hands the default's share to one predicted alternative: on sorting, under rules frozen before the data existed, bubble sort fell from 62% of programs to 0% and quicksort rose to 68%. Every passing program outside the labelled families that a blind judge checked was a known algorithm.

Entropy over algorithm families under steering, below the mean of five random directions of matched norm (sorting, redraw with fresh seeds; 95% CI [−0.95, −0.45]). Lower entropy means selection, not flattening.

Contributions

01

A preregistered test of selecting against flattening

Frozen predictions of which family gains, and a baseline of random directions averaged over draws, decide whether a steering direction spreads probability over known families or hands it to one.

02

Steering selects, replicated across draws and tasks

On a redraw with fresh seeds, the direction away from the mode gives quicksort 68% and A* 63% of programs, against 24% and 6% under random directions of matched norm.

03

A prompting ceiling for novelty

Steering toward families absent from the baseline produces them in 0% to 1.3% of programs for three of four targets. Naming them in the prompt produces them in 92% to 100%.

04

Behavioural context for the same conclusion

Temperature adds families slowly while correctness falls, random seed strings concentrate the choice, and negative instructions against never-written families reshuffle the ones the model does write.

Method

The unit of measurement is the idea: the algorithm family a program implements, checked by running it. A control that adds novelty should move programs onto families the model rarely writes, and those programs should still pass.

  1. Sample, label and execute

    The prompts are bare user turns: Implement a path search algorithm in Python and Implement a sorting algorithm. The baseline holds 300 programs per task at temperature 0.7, no top-k, a 768-token cap.

    Label
    A deterministic labeller assigns a known family (BFS, DFS, Dijkstra, A*, IDDFS, Bellman-Ford and others; bubble, quick, merge, selection, heap sort and others), plus other and unparseable. Against a blind LLM judge, Cohen's kappa is 0.84 [0.78, 0.89].
    Run
    A sandboxed oracle runs path search programs on 50 random directed graphs and 25 grids, and sorting programs on 50 lists. A program passes only if every test case is correct.

  2. Read the direction away from the mode

    For each program, the residual-stream activation is averaged over a commitment window: the 17 generated tokens starting at the first token that names or implements the idea, or the first 32 generated tokens when no such token exists. The direction is the difference in means between programs that do not and do implement the default family m, normalised to unit length.

    vℓ = meanx : idea(x) ≠ m hℓ(x) − meanx : idea(x) = m hℓ(x)

  3. Steer during generation

    The edit α · (r̄ℓ/3) · vℓ is added at layers 32 to 34 of 36, where r̄ℓ is the median residual norm, at the final prompt token and the first 64 generated tokens. A linear probe chose the band, but its accuracy was nearly flat across layers, so the band choice carries little information.

  4. Freeze the predictions, then generate

    Before any steered generation, two rankings of predicted gainers and two strengths were written to files whose SHA-256 hashes were recorded. The projection ranking put A* first on path search and quicksort first on sorting; a competing nearest neighbour ranking put IDDFS and selection sort first. Strength s1 is the α whose mode share is closest to half the baseline mode share; s2 is the α with the lowest mode share whose pass rate stays at least 0.8 times the baseline's, both calibrated on separate runs of 30 programs.

    SELECTAt s2 one non-default idea takes at least half the programs, it is a predicted gainer, and the distance from uniform rises again from s1 to s2.
    FLATTENThe distance from uniform falls from baseline to s1 to s2, and no non-default idea reaches half the programs.

  5. Compare against what else could explain the shift

    Each targeted setting is compared with the mean of five random directions of matched norm at the same strength, with a hierarchical bootstrap over draws and programs. Further comparators: the other task's direction, temperatures 1.0 and 1.3, directions toward families outside the menu, and a prompt that simply names the family.

Why the design looks like this

A steering vector of the right norm changes outputs whatever its direction, and the effect of one random direction varies widely from draw to draw. A targeted direction that raises diversity once is therefore weak evidence, which is why the baseline averages five draws.

Flattening and selecting predict opposite things for entropy relative to random directions of the same norm, and only selection predicts which family will gain. That makes the two outcomes separable with frozen rules.

Every steering condition shares one seeded sampler, so the same sample index sees the same random numbers in every condition. The HuggingFace baseline agrees with a vLLM baseline from the negative-instruction experiments (Jensen–Shannon divergence 0.009 on path search and 0.001 on sorting; code: Does steering pick ideas or spread them?).

Results

The frozen rule returns SELECT on both tasks and both draws. The sorting labels are the cleaner measurement (kappa 0.94 against a judge on steered outputs, against 0.39 on path search), so the headline comes from sorting.

The direction away from the mode selects one predicted idea

On sorting at s2 (α = 0.473), quicksort, the top prediction of the projection ranking, rises from 23.3% to 65.3% and 68.0%, and merge sort takes nearly all of the rest. On path search at s2 (α = 0.224), A*, again the top predicted gainer, rises from 8.0% to 56.7% and 63.3%. None of the five random directions makes A* the top gainer.

Selection on path search costs correctness: the pass rate falls to 0.17 on both draws, and 77% to 78% of outputs reach the token cap.

On sorting, entropy at s2 lies 0.71 bits below the random mean on the main draw ([−0.94, −0.44]) and 0.73 bits below on the redraw ([−0.95, −0.45]). A flattening direction would raise entropy above random perturbations of the same norm. This one concentrates the distribution on two families.

Path search shows the same selection with a different entropy signature. The non-mode share lies 0.54 above the random mean on the main draw ([0.47, 0.61]) and 0.53 above on the redraw ([0.45, 0.60]). Entropy is above the random mean on the main draw (+0.32 bits, [0.04, 0.56]) and undetermined on the redraw (+0.10, [−0.19, 0.36]): moving the majority to A* while three other families keep 7% to 15% each raises entropy even though one family takes over. Entropy alone would have called this a diversity gain.

Three checks bound the result. The nearest neighbour ranking was wrong on both tasks, so the direction lands on the family along its own projection, not on the one closest to the mode. The other task's direction also makes A* (26.7%) and quicksort (34.7%) the most common non-default families, though neither reaches half; part of the effect is a generic push away from the default. And random directions are not inert: on sorting, one random draw at s2 gives selection sort 48% and another gives quicksort 50%, and the random mode share ranges from 0.19 to 0.69. A comparison with any single random cell would have misled in either direction.

The same selection under a different prompt and layer band

A second experiment on the same model fixed the function signature and output format, which narrowed path search to two families (BFS 98 and DFS 44 of 150 programs, pass rate 0.673). Its direction, taken over the first 32 generated tokens at layers 29 to 31, had been registered as a flattening control. At α = 0.224 it produced DFS in 145 of 150 programs at an unchanged pass rate (0.680), and idea entropy fell from 1.175 to 0.235 bits. Three random directions of matched norm at the same strength left the split near baseline (BFS 44 to 50, DFS 21 to 31 of 75; code: Can internal knobs beat temperature for novel code?). This is exploratory evidence: the selection was observed after the flattening test it was designed for had failed.

Selection does not reach new ideas

If steering only selects among families the model already writes, it should fail to produce families it never writes. Directions toward the centroids of families outside the menu, built from programs the model wrote when asked for them, were compared with simply asking.

Three of four directions fail. The IDDFS direction moves mass from BFS to DFS (35.3%) and the Bellman-Ford direction moves it to Dijkstra (34.7%), the nearest families the model already writes. Heap sort is the exception. Naming the family works for all four.

The broader notion of novelty fares no better. In the two-family experiment, a blind judge labelled every passing program in the labeller's residual class, and all 70 were known algorithms. Asking for novelty in that experiment's prompt did no better: a request for a new algorithm left 136 of 150 path search programs on BFS or DFS, and a request for a fully novel algorithm of 2,000 words cut the pass rate from 0.67 to 0.11. Concept erasure with LEACE cut the balanced accuracy of a linear idea probe from 0.888 to 0.334 (chance 0.333) without moving the outputs off the known families. On textbook tasks this metric is near its floor by construction, so the zero bounds novelty in this setting rather than in general.

Temperature adds ideas slowly and breaks code quickly

The temperature and seed experiments used vLLM with 100 programs per cell, sampling temperatures T from 0.3 to 1.6, top-k 10 or none, and a 1,536-token cap on the redraw (code: Does temperature or a seed phrase change the algorithm an LLM picks?).

The model's default is a mode, not an average. Even at T = 0.3 with top-k 10, ten path search programs contain about 3.5 ideas written in about 9 distinct phrasings. Ten ideas with one phrasing each never occurs in any cell; the largest expected count among ten is 4.9. On the weighted shortest path prompt the model writes Dijkstra's algorithm in 98 to 100 of 100 programs up to T = 1.0.

Prompt context also selects rather than diversifies

Prepending a random seed string in the format of seed=d32qxk4nt43 (60 strings of random characters such as seed=i9609s2lg7o and 60 lists of four random words such as seed=(mask bed square finger); the two literal example strings were not among them) lowers idea entropy at T = 0.7 by 0.34 bits on path search ([−0.53, −0.13]) and 0.68 bits on sorting ([−0.92, −0.47]), over 120 strings with 5 samples each against 300 unseeded programs. On sorting, random words send 282 of 300 programs to bubble sort. This narrowing is exploratory: the preregistered prediction of seed effects failed.

Negative instructions (Do not use X.) act as instructions to choose something else, not as removal of an option. Removing an option should never lower the share of one that remains. In a preregistered test on 5,930 confirmation programs, negative instructions lowered the share of a family they did not name in 8 of 46 tested cells, spread over 6 negative instructions. Placebo blocks compared with each other gave 0 such cells out of 45 (code: Do idea bans steer an LLM's internal state?).

The internal account of negative instructions is partial. Adding a negative instruction's displacement to a placebo run recovers none of the effect (mean recovery −0.001, against −0.006 for random displacements). The logit lens of the displacement shows tokens such as while, another and rather, and ranks the banned name about 17,600th, so the final prompt state reads as an instruction to do something else. On gemma-2-2b-it, regularity violations were rare (1 of 72 cells).

What this means for evaluating diversity

On path search, an evaluation that scored diversity by entropy against a single random direction would have reported a diversity gain; the family-level view shows a switch of default with the pass rate halved. The paper draws two practices for steering studies that claim diversity or creativity gains: report what fraction of the gain goes to one family, and compare with the mean of several random directions of matched norm rather than one.

Limitations

  • One model, two prompts. The selection result is measured on Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct and two prompts, each with one wording. The steering test was not run on gemma-2-2b-it, and the negative-instruction results there did not reproduce the Qwen pattern, so the claim does not extend to other families or scales without new evidence.
  • A loosened rule and a missed bar. The SELECT rule accepted the top prediction of either of two frozen rankings, which loosens it. On path search the s2 operating point missed the frozen pass-rate bar (0.17 against 0.25).
  • Layer band and window. The band was chosen by a probe whose accuracy was flat across layers, and the window covers the first 64 generated tokens. Other bands or windows may flatten where this one selects.
  • Path search labels are noisy. The labeller agrees with a blind judge at kappa 0.39 on steered path search outputs, so the exact A* share is uncertain, even though its rise from 8% to 57% and 63% far exceeds that error. The sorting result, at kappa 0.94, is the cleaner of the two.
  • Correctness cost and truncation. Steering on path search halved the pass rate and pushed 77% to 78% of outputs to the token cap, a cost the frozen calibration did not predict. Continuing 150 truncated programs flipped 10.7% of labels in the temperature experiments, and batch composition alone flips 11% to 17% of labels in repeated runs.
  • Novelty near zero by construction. Novelty was measured as correct code outside known algorithm families, which is near zero on textbook tasks. Only the two-family experiment had a judge check the passing programs in the labeller's residual class; 11 such programs from the later experiments were not judged. The result says these controls did not create new algorithms for path search and sorting, not that steering cannot raise novelty on open problems.
  • Exploratory pieces. The two-family experiment is exploratory evidence for selection, and the seed narrowing, the attention knockout and the edge statistic are exploratory too.
  • The commitment point is not located. Probes on the first eight generated tokens were at chance, and neither forking-path resampling nor other token-level attribution by resampling was run. A sparse autoencoder analysis on gemma-2-2b-it reconstructed activations poorly (fraction of variance unexplained 0.61) and supports no claim.
